Decision · ADR-0115

The owl's voice and ear come from speech models behind a port Accepted

  • Context
  • Decision
  • Addendum (2026-11-28) — the spoken conversation
  • Consequences
  • Status: Accepted
  • Date: 2026-11-28
  • Deciders: NexusHero (Product Owner), Steuereule Engineering
  • Applies to: apps/api/src/speech/, apps/api/src/capabilities/, apps/mobile-web/src/lib/readAloud/, apps/mobile-web/src/lib/conversation/, apps/mobile-web/src/screens/advisor/conversation/
  • Builds on: ADR-0038 (the model sits behind a port), ADR-0109 (cost limits on costly routes), ADR-0111 (production configuration is declared)

#Context

„Vorlesen“ under an answer read with the device's own voice (Web Speech API). That costs nothing and sends nothing, but the voice is whatever the device ships, and a device without a voice for the language has no button at all. Dictation and a spoken conversation with the owl are planned; both need speech from a model, and the Product Owner chose a model voice over the device voice by ear from samples.

The chat model does not have to be the speech model. The user picks the chat model per conversation (ADR-0096); a speech model only turns finished text into audio. Anthropic offers no speech endpoint, so a conversation answered by Claude needs another provider for its voice anyway.

#Decision

  1. A port, independent of the chat model. SpeechPort.speak({ text, language }) returns one WAV file. Two adapters: MistralSpeech (Voxtral, POST /v1/audio/speech, data hosted in the EEA by default) and GeminiSpeech (Generative Language API). FakeSpeech is the scripted voice of CI and tests, on with AI_FAKE_KI. NoSpeech stands where nothing is configured. Another provider is another adapter. Which model answers a conversation plays no part in which provider speaks it.
  2. Off until named. The voice is on only with AI_SPEECH_PROVIDER (mistral or google-genai) and that provider's own key (MISTRAL_API_KEY; GEMINI_API_KEY or GOOGLE_API_KEY). A key that is present for the chat does not switch it on: reading aloud sends answer text to the speech provider, which is a data flow of its own and may go to a provider the chat does not use. AI_KILL_SWITCH switches it off with every other model slot.
  3. One route, text in. POST /v1/eulen-modus/stimme takes one passage of at most 600 characters and a language (de, en). It needs an account, refuses assistant tokens, and answers audio/wav with Cache-Control: no-store. The app converts an answer to spoken text (speechText) and sends it in passages of at most 220 characters, so the first passage plays while the next is fetched. The route takes text rather than a message id because a spoken conversation will speak sentences of an answer that is still being written.
  4. Limits. 150 passages per account in ten minutes (SPEECH_THROTTLE), and AI_SPEECH_DAILY_CALL_LIMIT (default 20,000) across all users. The daily counter is separate from the chat's model-call budget, so listening does not use up answers.
  5. The app asks the deployment. GET /v1/capabilities carries eulenStimme. On, „Vorlesen“ uses the server voice and needs no voice on the device. Off, it reads with the device's voice as before. When the server voice fails in the middle of an answer, the device's voice reads the rest where there is one.
  6. No provider text in logs or errors. A failed call is logged with its reason and HTTP status only, because a provider's error message may quote the passage. The client gets a German sentence and a code (SPEECH_UNAVAILABLE, SPEECH_FAILED, SPEECH_BUDGET_SPENT).
  7. Voice and model. Defaults per provider: Mistral voxtral-mini-tts-3-0 with voice_481 (Heidi, its one German preset) and voice_447 for English, because a Mistral voice speaks one language; Gemini gemini-3.8-flash-tts with Sulafat for both. All are settings (AI_SPEECH_MODEL, AI_SPEECH_VOICE, AI_SPEECH_VOICE_EN). The request carries the passage and nothing else: a style line in front of it was spoken with it (checked by transcribing the audio).

#Addendum (2026-11-28) — the spoken conversation

  1. Hearing. SpeechPort.transcribe({ wav, language }) answers the words of one utterance. POST /v1/eulen-modus/gehoert takes it as a multipart WAV file (16-bit mono, at most 2 MB, about 60 seconds), with its own throttle (120 in ten minutes) and the daily counter of the voice. The recording is not kept. Gemini gets the recording next to an order to write it down and not to act on it; Mistral gets it at its transcription endpoint.

  2. Silence never reaches a model. Asked to write down two seconds of silence, Gemini answered "Hallo, guten Morgen." SpeechService checks the samples first (hasSound: three 50 ms frames above -40 dBFS) and answers an empty text.

  3. A cascade, not a speech-to-speech model. Utterance → words → an ordinary chat message → answer → voice. The owl's tools, the tax core, the topic guard and the model the user picked all stay in the path, and everything said is a stored chat message. The price is latency: each step waits for the one before.

  4. The course of a conversation is one framework-free state machine (lib/conversation/conversation.ts): off → starting → listening ⇄ hearing → understanding → thinking → speaking → listening. An utterance ends after 0.9 s of quiet (voiceActivity.ts) or with „Fertig“. The answer is spoken while it is written: its first sentence alone, then passages of at most 220 characters. Only the owl's own written words are spoken; notes the app appends to the stored answer stay in the chat.

  5. Interrupting is a press. While the owl speaks the microphone is not evaluated, because it would hear the owl's own voice. „Unterbrechen“ stops the voice and the answer.

  6. Spoken answers are short. A spoken message carries spoken: true. The turn context then asks for at most three short sentences without lists or headings and one question at most, and the model thinks at its lowest level (withSpokenPace), unless the message asked for Max. Measured with Gemini 3.8 Flash: without it the owl spoke for almost three minutes and began after 22 seconds; with it, two sentences, the first after 11 to 18 seconds.

  7. The picture is a line. Straight at rest; the two lighter greys follow the microphone, all three follow the voice (from the loudness of the WAV file that plays), one bump travels while the owl thinks. No accent colour. With reduced motion the line stands still and the state is written under it, as it always is.

  8. It starts with a press that says what leaves the device. The microphone is opened only after „Gespräch starten“, under the sentence that what is said goes to the speech service.

  9. What did not go to the chat model is struck through in what was said (ADR-0091, addendum). The note under it says that the speech service heard it: the masking runs after the transcription, before the chat model.

#Consequences

  • With the voice on, the text of every answer a user has read aloud goes to the speech provider. Personal data reaches an answer only with the user's consent (ADR-0091), but then it is in the spoken text too. The provider has to be named in the privacy notice before AI_SPEECH_PROVIDER is set on a deployment. Mistral states that API data is hosted in the EEA by default; whether inputs are used for training is a setting of the Mistral account (Admin, Privacy) and has to be off, and zero data retention is granted on request for paid accounts. A Generative Language API key gives no assurance of processing in the EU.
  • The Mistral adapter is tested against the documented request and response, not yet against the live API: no Mistral key existed when it was written. The real-call measurements below are Gemini's.
  • Audio is sent as uncompressed WAV, about 48 kB per second at 24 kHz. A passage is a few hundred kilobytes. A compressed format would need an encoder on the server.
  • A spoken conversation is slower than one with a speech-to-speech model: measured, 3 to 4 seconds until the words stand on screen (0.9 s pause, then the transcription) and 11 to 18 seconds until the first spoken word, most of it the chat model. Streaming transcription and a streamed voice would shorten the first and the last step; the wait for the chat model stays.
  • Not checked on a real microphone, on Safari or on a phone: the gate and the measurements fed the browser a synthetic microphone. Echo, room noise and the pause threshold need a hearing test.
  • The speech model is not streamed: a passage plays once it is complete. The wait before the first word is the time the provider needs for the first passage, measured at 1.9 to 3.8 seconds for passages of 3 to 139 characters (seven calls, 28.11.2026).
  • Dictation through the server is not built. The composer's microphone still writes down on the device (ADR-0061).
